I keep coming back to SeaWorld San Antonio, and I keep leaving with the same thought. This park has the space, the weather, and the bones to be one of the best in Texas. It just hasn't fully decided to be.

Let me walk through what I mean, because I don't think this is a knock. It's closer to the opposite.

## The land is just sitting there

The first thing you notice when you actually walk the property is how much room there is. Other parks are packing coasters into parking lots and squeezing new layouts around old ones. San Antonio doesn't have that problem. There's open ground here, flat and ready, and it's been that way for a while.

That's the part that gets me. The hard constraint most parks fight — where do we even put it — isn't really a constraint here.

## What's already good

Wave Breaker runs well and fits the setting. Great White still holds up as an inverted coaster even after all these years. And the park itself is pleasant to walk around in a way that's easy to undervalue until you've been somewhere that isn't.

So this isn't a park that's broken. It's a park that's comfortable. Those are different things, and the second one is trickier, because comfortable parks don't feel any urgency to change.

## Where it could go

Here's the wishlist part, and I'll keep it grounded because guessing wild is the easy version of this.

A major coaster on that open land is the obvious move. Not a re-theme, not a flat ride cluster — a headliner people drive in for. The market's there. San Antonio pulls from a big region, and right now a lot of that region drives past on the way to somewhere else.

I'd also love to see them lean into what makes the park specifically theirs instead of chasing what other parks already did five years ago. The animal side of SeaWorld is a real differentiator when it's treated as one.

## The honest read

I'm not writing this because I'm frustrated with the park. I'm writing it because I like it enough to want more from it. There's a version of SeaWorld San Antonio that becomes a destination instead of a stop, and most of the pieces for that version are already on the ground.

They just have to make the move.
