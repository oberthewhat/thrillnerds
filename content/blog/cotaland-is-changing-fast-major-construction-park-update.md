COTALand is starting to feel less like a construction site and much more like a finished theme park.

I recently had the chance to film inside the park, and the biggest thing that stood out was not any single ride. It was how quickly all the smaller details were changing the property.

Full-grown trees are being planted. New concrete pathways are appearing. Shade structures are going up around rides, plazas, and walkways. The landscaping is filling in areas that looked completely different only a few days earlier.

There is still work to finish before opening day, but this latest COTALand construction update gave me a much clearer picture of what Austin's new amusement park will actually feel like.

## COTALand is putting real effort into shade and landscaping

Shade has clearly been part of the COTALand design from the beginning.

That matters when you are building an outdoor amusement park in Austin, Texas. A good ride collection can bring people through the gate, but trees, covered seating, and shaded pathways can decide how long families are comfortable staying during a Texas summer.

COTALand has purchased mature trees and plants instead of waiting years for young landscaping to grow in. That decision is already helping the park feel established.

Covered areas are appearing throughout the property, including around the Grand Carousel, along pathways, and near places where guests will stop to eat or listen to live music.

The park still has fresh concrete, construction equipment, and unfinished sections, but the completed areas are beginning to connect. Landscaping is doing a lot of the work.

## The safari ride is becoming a complete family attraction

The safari ride has changed considerably since my previous visit.

The trail is surrounded by plants, and the attraction now looks and feels much closer to the jungle adventure COTALand has been describing. The small safari jeeps will travel past animal animatronics, including giraffes, elephants, and lions.

There is also a cave featuring bats as a nod to Austin's famous bat population.

It is a smart local detail. COTALand is located at Circuit of the Americas, but the park does not need every attraction to be about racing. Small Austin references can give the family areas their own identity without making the theming feel forced.

## Soggy Logger is getting close to testing

Soggy Logger is one of the areas that impressed me most during this visit.

The log flume is surrounded by trees and themed details, and its ride vehicles were already waiting nearby. The team expected to begin adding water soon, which would allow logs to start moving through the course for testing.

This is also an important attraction for the overall COTALand ride lineup. Austin summers are hot, and a family water ride gives guests a way to cool down without leaving the theme park.

Water Mania will provide another place to get wet in the middle of the park. I would not be surprised to see COTALand add more water attractions as the park grows.

## The ride collection is packed into the park

One thing I like about the COTALand layout is how closely the attractions are placed together.

The park is using its current footprint well. Instead of long empty walks between rides, guests will be surrounded by movement in several directions.

The Screamin' Swing was already cycling during the visit. These are some of my favorite flat rides, and watching it run makes that section of the park feel much closer to opening.

The Starflyer was also nearly ready to operate, while the Telecombat ride was in place nearby. Telecombat lets riders control their movement and try to knock other vehicles out of the sky, which should make it one of the more interactive rides in the park.

COTALand is also using full-size rides alongside smaller children's versions. A large pirate ship will sit near a smaller pirate ship, and a new Mini Miami will be placed next to the children's model.

That setup gives adults, older children, and younger riders similar experiences in the same area instead of separating the family across the park.

## Big Air is the next major ride to watch

The site for the Big Air roller coaster was active during this update, with vertical construction expected to begin soon.

Once the track starts going up, it should come together quickly and make an immediate visual difference inside COTALand.

Big Air will add another roller coaster to a lineup already anchored by Circuit Breaker and Palindrome. It also helps round out a park that is trying to offer more than a collection of carnival rides.

COTALand is developing into a real Austin theme park with roller coasters, family rides, water attractions, children's rides, food, live entertainment, and seasonal events all inside one destination.

## The entrance plaza is taking shape

The COTALand entrance plaza still had plenty of activity around it, but the facades were being installed across the buildings.

This is where many of the final details become especially noticeable. Shops, fencing, signs, plants, pavement, and finished building fronts may not require the same construction schedule as a roller coaster, but they determine whether guests feel like they have entered a complete park.

The Ferris wheel is also moving closer to completion. Its gondolas still need to be installed, and the control system is being changed before the ride can begin operating.

There is visible work left, particularly around the front of the park. A lot of it now appears to be the kind of finishing work that can change quickly from one visit to the next.

## Parking and transportation are part of the opening plan

COTALand plans to offer both premium parking and spaces farther from the park entrance.

Transportation will be available between the more distant parking area and the entrance. Guests will still be able to walk, but that extra option will be welcome during the hottest part of the year.

A parking toll booth is also planned as part of the completed arrival experience.

These details are easy to overlook while the rides receive most of the attention, but parking flow will be important once COTALand begins welcoming regular crowds at Circuit of the Americas.

## Seasonal events should give people reasons to return

COTALand is already discussing events that can change the atmosphere of the park throughout the year.

Peppermint Parkway is expected to evolve into a park-based holiday event called Peppermint in the Park. Halloween programming and other seasonal events are also part of the plan.

That could be especially valuable for local season passholders. COTALand does not have to depend entirely on tourists visiting Austin or guests attending a race at COTA. Holiday events, new entertainment, and seasonal decorations can give Central Texas families a reason to return throughout the year.

## Phase 2 is already being discussed

The immediate priority is getting COTALand open, training the team, and bringing the rides online.

The park is also thinking about Phase 2.

Specific attractions have not been announced, and there is no reason to guess at projects before COTALand is ready to share them. What we can see is that the property has room to grow beyond the opening-day footprint.

More information about the next phase could begin to emerge toward the end of August. How much land COTALand uses and which type of attraction comes next will tell us a lot about the park's long-term direction.

## COTALand could become an important park for Austin

People have been asking when COTALand will open for a long time. I understand the impatience, especially after following this project through years of construction.

After seeing the work happening inside the park, I also understand why the team is taking the time to finish these areas properly.

The mature landscaping, shaded paths, themed buildings, and mix of attractions are creating a more complete experience than some people expect when they hear that a new amusement park is opening beside a racetrack.

COTALand can give Austin and the surrounding Central Texas communities something they have not had: a nearby park where families can make a day trip, use a season pass, ride roller coasters, and return for special events without driving to San Antonio or Arlington.

There is still plenty to finish. The difference is that it is now much easier to see the park waiting underneath all that construction.
